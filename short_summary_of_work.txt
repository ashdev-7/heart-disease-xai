1. THE WHOLE THING IN ONE PAGE
================================================================================

Question we ended up answering
  When a heart-disease risk model explains one patient's prediction, how much
  of that explanation is fixed by the patient, and how much changes with
  (a) the model class, (b) the explanation method, (c) the training sample,
  (d) the random seed, (e) ordinary measurement noise, (f) the explainer's
  background sample?

Material
  Two datasets : Framingham (4,240 people, 10-year coronary heart disease)
                 BRFSS 2015 (253,680 survey respondents, existing heart disease)
  Four models  : logistic regression (LR), random forest (RF), XGBoost (XGB),
                 neural network (MLP, 64-32)
  Explainers   : exact linear SHAP (LR), TreeSHAP (RF, XGB), Integrated
                 Gradients and Expected Gradients (MLP), KernelSHAP (all four)
  Retrains     : Framingham 10 seeds + 100 bootstraps; BRFSS 10 seeds + 50
                 bootstraps; every retrain explained for the same 500 patients
  References   : D'Agostino 2008 risk equation, INTERHEART 2004

Answer in five lines
  1. Explanations are FAITHFUL. Replacing a patient's top-3 features moves the
     prediction 2.2-3.3 times more than replacing 3 random features.
  2. Explanations are PLAUSIBLE. Grouped importance matches the published risk
     equation (cosine 0.90-0.96; random assignment scores about 0.65-0.74).
  3. The ORDER of a patient's leading features is NOT REPRODUCIBLE on small
     data. On Framingham a patient's top-3 features agree only 0.52-0.70
     between two bootstrap retrains (chance = 0.13). The DIRECTION of those
     features (raises or lowers risk) is stable: same sign 96-100% of the time.
  4. That instability is mostly a sample-size effect. Shrinking BRFSS to
     Framingham's size reproduces it; accuracy barely changes meanwhile.
  5. Which MODEL you use changes the explanation more than which EXPLAINER you
     use. SMOTE damages calibration and changes explanations.

The draft paper's two headline claims do not survive: "no method is clinically
aligned" came from the hand-built reference, and "exact SHAP is worse than
approximate SHAP" came from comparing different models trained with SMOTE.

2. WHERE WE STARTED
================================================================================

The draft
  Title   : Evaluating the Reliability and Clinical Consistency of
            Explainability Methods in Deep Learning-Based Heart Disease
            Prediction
  Design  : LR, RF, MLP trained with SMOTE; SHAP and Integrated Gradients;
            three tests (stability under 1/2/5% noise on 30 patients,
            agreement across 10 seeds, Kendall tau against a ranking built from
            ACC/AHA 2019 recommendation classes); pass/fail verdicts.
  Claims  : no method reaches clinical alignment (tau >= 0.60);
            MLP-SHAP (approximate) beats RF-SHAP (exact);
            explanations are consistent across seeds (rho > 0.95) yet unstable
            per patient ("dissociation");
            "first systematic, pre-specified framework".

Eleven problems found on review (2026-10-03)
   1  The clinical reference used guideline recommendation classes as if they
      were a ranking of predictive importance. They measure strength of
      evidence for treating a factor.
   2  Kendall tau on 15 features with 8 tied; no confidence interval, no
      chance level. One verdict turned on 0.395 against a 0.40 cutoff.
   3  "Approximate beats exact" compared RF+TreeSHAP with MLP+KernelSHAP:
      model and explainer both changed.
   4  The "dissociation" compared a global ranking with per-patient rankings.
   5  Counterfactuals (DiCE) announced, no results; the notebook only
      installed the package.
   6  "Pre-registered" with no registration; one threshold was changed after
      seeing the models.
   7  Seed test run on Framingham only, yet "replicates on both datasets".
   8  Stability metric averaged rank shift over all features (tail-dominated).
   9  Agreement with logistic regression used as if LR were the truth.
  10  Weak models, one small MLP, no XGBoost, single split, SMOTE never tested.
  11  No test that explanations reflect the model at all (faithfulness).
  Plus citation errors (Krishna et al. 2022 mis-titled and mis-described).

S5. PHASE 2 - REFERENCE CONSTRUCTION      (src/phase2_reference.py)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  [D'Agostino 2008 coefficients]     [Framingham rows, age 30-74, no stroke]
            |                                   |
            +---------> apply equation, untrained (4,146 rows)
                               |
                               +--> D4 AUC, calibration by tenth  --> STOP RULE 2
                               +--> refit same form, compare coefficients
                               v
            Ref-A: exact Shapley values of the equation on our data
                   (7 inputs, 128 coalitions, 200 background rows)
            Ref-B: lipid-model coefficient x SD of the variable
  [INTERHEART 2004 Table 3] --> Ref-C (Framingham groups), Ref-D (BRFSS items)

  Feature groups (Framingham): Age; Sex; Blood pressure (sysBP, diaBP,
    prevalentHyp, BPMeds); Smoking (currentSmoker, cigsPerDay); Glycaemia
    (diabetes, glucose); Adiposity (BMI); Lipids (totChol); Unreferenced
    (education, heartRate, prevalentStroke).

  Expectation: equation AUC 0.65-0.75; predicted risk ABOVE observed, because
               the equation predicts all cardiovascular disease and the target
               is heart disease only.
  Outcome    : PASS on discrimination: AUC 0.718 (0.695-0.739); 0.712 in men and
               in women. Predicted and observed rise together across tenths
               (Kendall tau 0.96).
               Calibration expectation WRONG: observed 14.8% is above predicted
               12.4% (ratio 1.20). Cause not established; consistent with an
               older, higher-risk cohort (49% current smokers).
               Refit: ln age 3.21 vs published 2.92; ln systolic BP 2.40 vs
               2.33; diabetes 0.71 vs 0.66; smoking 0.39 vs 0.66; BMI not
               significant.
     Ref-A importance shares: Age 28%, Sex 23%, Smoking 22%, Blood pressure
               20%, Adiposity 5%, Glycaemia 2%.
     Ref-A vs Ref-B cosine 0.99, but both come from the same paper.
  Finding not anticipated: INTERHEART ranks diabetes 3rd of 4 by odds ratio but
     last by attributable risk. Mean |SHAP| is a population measure, so it must
     be compared with attributable risk, not with odds ratios. Ref-C and Ref-D
     were changed accordingly.
  Finding not anticipated: a published 7-input equation with no training
     (0.718) does as well as every model in the draft (0.666-0.698).

  INTERHEART values, read from the paper itself (fully adjusted, 99% CI):
     ApoB/ApoA1 3.25, psychosocial 2.67, diabetes 2.37, smoking current+former
     2.04 (current 2.87), hypertension 1.91, abdominal obesity 1.62, fruit and
     vegetables 0.70, exercise 0.86, alcohol 0.91. Hypertension and diabetes
     are self-reported. BMI and alcohol were dropped from the BRFSS mapping
     after reading the paper (BMI non-significant next to waist/hip ratio;
     alcohol is a different exposure).

S7. PHASE 3 - MODELS AND TRAINING VARIANTS   (src/common.py, phase3_models.py)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  [dataset] --stratified 80/20 split (seed 42)--> train | test
  [test]    --stratified draw--> 500 explained patients (fixed)
  [train]   --random draw-----> 100 background rows (fixed, real data)

  [train] --5-fold CV on log loss--> hyperparameters (tuned ONCE)
          --fit--> BASE models (seed 42)
          --refit, seeds 0-9--> 10 SEED variants (RF, XGB, MLP)
          --resample rows with replacement, refit--> BOOTSTRAP variants
                                                    (100 Framingham, 50 BRFSS)
  In every variant: imputation (median; mode for yes/no columns) and
  standardisation are refitted on that variant's training rows. No resampling
  for class imbalance.

  Chosen hyperparameters
    Framingham: LR C=0.1; RF 300 trees, leaf 5, depth 12; XGB depth 2, 100
                trees, rate 0.05; MLP 62 epochs
    BRFSS     : LR C=0.1; RF leaf 50, depth 12; XGB depth 4, 300 trees;
                MLP 18 epochs

  Expectation: on Framingham LR at least as good as the flexible models (about
               34 events per predictor; van der Ploeg 2014 says flexible models
               need over 10 times more than LR).
  Outcome (base models, test set)
                 Framingham AUC (95% CI)     slope     BRFSS AUC (95% CI)   slope
     LR          0.702 (0.652-0.746)         0.79      0.847 (0.841-0.852)  0.99
     MLP         0.696 (0.647-0.740)         0.75      0.849 (0.843-0.853)  1.10
     XGB         0.684 (0.632-0.732)         0.83      0.851 (0.846-0.856)  0.99
     RF          0.665 (0.614-0.713)         0.69      0.848 (0.843-0.853)  1.19
     As expected. On BRFSS all four are within 0.004 AUC.

  Where it ran: Framingham on the laptop. BRFSS on Kaggle, because the laptop
     (7.6 GB RAM) had its jobs stopped three times for low memory. Library
     versions on Kaggle were verified from the run log: scikit-learn 1.8.0,
     xgboost 3.1.3, shap 0.52.0, numpy 2.2.6 (identical to the laptop);
     torch 2.11.0 vs 2.9.1 (not pinned). Kaggle run took 5.2 hours.

  Expectation: no prior expectation recorded for size; the draft had no such

  Outcome    : gap_top3 / gap_random3
                 Framingham 2.2 (RF) to 3.0 (XGB); top beats random in 97-100%

                 BRFSS 3.0 to 3.3; 99-100% of patients
               Bottom-3 gap is near zero for native explainers.
               All explainers are faithful and about equally so.

S10. AXIS R - ROBUSTNESS TO MEASUREMENT NOISE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  Framingham
  [patient row] --add noise (20 draws)--> re-explain --> compare top-3 with
                                                         the clean explanation
     noise per feature from published within-person variation:
       systolic and diastolic BP 8.6% (Warren 2010, office readings 6 weeks
       apart); cholesterol 5.95%, glucose 5.6% (Ricos database); heart rate
       3 bpm (Quer 2020). "Low" level = half. Age, BMI, cigarettes and yes/no

  BRFSS
     change rates calibrated so a simulated re-interview reproduces published
     agreement (Andresen 2003): GenHlth kappa 0.75 -> 21% change rate;
     PhysHlth 0.71 -> 29%; MentHlth 0.67 -> 33%. Other items held fixed.

  Expectation: the draft reported RF as the least stable; expected again.
  Outcome (top-3 agreement, clean vs re-measured)
                         Framingham high   low      BRFSS re-interview
     LR  linear SHAP     0.846             0.919    0.950
     MLP Expected Grad.  0.832             0.905    0.942
     XGB TreeSHAP        0.829             0.902    0.933
     MLP Integr. Grad.   0.818             0.895    0.945
     RF  TreeSHAP        0.673             0.783    0.918
     Predicted class flips in 4-8% of re-measurements (Framingham, 20% risk

S15. SAMPLE-SIZE EXPERIMENT (exploratory)     (src/phase6_sample_size.py)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  [BRFSS train] --fixed stratified subsample of n rows-->
     re-tune hyperparameters --> 30 bootstraps of the subsample -->
     explain the same 200 patients (same background) --> top-3 agreement

  Expectation: agreement falls as n falls; at Framingham's events-per-predictor
               ratio BRFSS should look like Framingham.
  Outcome    : as expected.
     events per predictor   15      34      135     910
     training rows          3,392   7,580   30,000  202,944
     LR  linear SHAP        0.750   0.746   0.868   0.957
     MLP Expected Grad.     0.667   0.670   0.817   0.888
     MLP Integr. Grad.      0.635   0.634   0.791   0.884
     RF  TreeSHAP           0.615   0.702   0.738   0.859
     XGB TreeSHAP           0.585   0.634   0.736   0.852
     Test AUC over the same range: 0.828-0.837 -> 0.847-0.850.
     At 34 events per predictor BRFSS gives 0.63-0.75; Framingham itself gives
     0.52-0.70.
  Replication (2026-10-05): with 3, 3 and 2 independently drawn subsamples at
     the three smaller sizes, the MEAN agreement rises at every step for all
     five rows (LR 0.72 / 0.79 / 0.87 / 0.96; XGB 0.60 / 0.65 / 0.74 / 0.85).
     One subsample can move a cell by about 0.10 at small sizes, so the flat
     step from 15 to 34 above is subsample noise. Bootstrapping one sample was
     also checked against drawing disjoint new samples: no systematic gap.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
S16. VARIANCE DECOMPOSITION                   (src/phase6_variance.py)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  [attribution] --> share = |phi_j| / sum |phi_j|   (per explanation)
  For ONE source at a time (others held at base), 100 patients:
     one-way ANOVA per feature, patients as groups
     between-patient variance, within-patient (source) variance, summed over
     features
     ICC = between / (between + within)
  ICC near 1: explanations differ because patients differ.
  ICC low  : the source moves explanations about as much as patients differ.

  Outcome (median ICC across model/explainer combinations)
     source                        Framingham   BRFSS
     model class                   0.67         0.84
     training-data bootstrap       0.68         0.98
     measurement noise             0.85         0.91
     background sample             0.96         0.96
     explainer (native vs kernel)  0.96         1.00
     seed                          0.96         0.99
  Not a full crossed design: interactions between sources are not estimated.
  Note: the background sample looks small here (0.96) but changed top-3
     membership in 7-20% of cases (S13). Both are true: small shifts in share
     flip features that are nearly tied in rank.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
S17. PER-PATIENT LINKING                      (src/phase6_shares_linking.py)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  per patient: bootstrap agreement, noise agreement, faithfulness ratio,
     plausibility, predicted risk, distance from threshold, prediction SD
     --> Spearman correlations across 500 patients

  Expectation: patients near the decision threshold have less stable
               explanations.
  Outcome    : mostly NOT supported.
     Framingham: bootstrap agreement vs distance from the 20% threshold
        0.08-0.23, except RF 0.44. Bootstrap vs noise agreement 0.39-0.60.
        Faithfulness and plausibility vs stability: below 0.22.
     BRFSS: bootstrap vs noise 0.05-0.40; bootstrap agreement falls with
        prediction SD (rho -0.27 to -0.41, except XGB +0.04).
     Reading: a noise test (no retraining needed) partly flags patients whose
     explanations will not reproduce, on the small dataset. Descriptive only;
     many correlations were computed.

6. WHAT THE DRAFT CLAIMED AGAINST WHAT THE DATA SHOW
================================================================================

  Draft: no method reaches clinical alignment (tau < 0.60).
  Data : with correlated features grouped and a reference that measures
         importance, every model matches the published equation (cosine
         0.90-0.96 against a random level of about 0.7) and all rank
         Age > Blood pressure > Smoking.

  Draft: approximate SHAP on the MLP beats exact SHAP on the RF.
  Data : KernelSHAP and the native explainer agree closely on the same model
         (0.95-1.00 for LR, XGB, MLP-EG). The difference the draft saw is
         between MODELS. The RF's odd ranking (education 4th) appears with
         SMOTE and not without it.

  Draft: explanations are consistent across retraining (rho > 0.95).
  Data : reproduced for seed-only retraining (0.96-0.99). Under bootstrap of
         the training data, a Framingham patient's top-3 agrees 0.52-0.70 and
         global top-3 agrees 0.63-0.75.

  Draft: "dissociation" between population consistency and patient stability.
  Data : once both are measured at the same level and under the same kind of
         retraining, there is no dissociation to explain; the per-patient
         instability tracks training-set size.

  Draft: MLP-IG stability depends on the proportion of yes/no features.
  Data : not tested by us in that form. The Framingham-BRFSS difference in
         noise robustness cannot be compared in size because the noise models
         differ.

  Draft: verdicts TRUSTWORTHY / USE WITH CAUTION / DO NOT USE.
  Data : no verdicts are issued. Results are reported with chance levels and
         confidence intervals.

8. LIMITATIONS
================================================================================

  Design
   - One train/test split per dataset. Performance has confidence intervals,
     but everything is conditional on that split.
   - Hyperparameters tuned once. Leak-free re-tuning per bootstrap was tested
     on Framingham and changes nothing material; not tested on BRFSS.
   - Variance decomposition is one source at a time; no interactions.
   - Sample-size experiment and background-sample factor were added after the
     protocol freeze. One subsample per size.
   - No clinician judged any explanation.
  Data
   - The Kaggle Framingham file has no formal codebook; HDL cholesterol is
     absent; the outcome label could not be checked against the cohort.
   - BRFSS is a cross-sectional survey of existing disease; BRFSS was not
     rebuilt from the raw CDC file.
   - Glucose is 9.2% imputed on Framingham; the planned complete-case
     sensitivity check has NOT been run.
  Reference
   - Ref-A and Ref-B come from one paper; the only independent reference
     (INTERHEART) shares 3-4 groups and uses different lipid measures.
   - The risk equation predicts all cardiovascular disease; the target is
     coronary heart disease.
   - Only 6-7 feature groups, so cosine is high by default.
  Noise models
   - Framingham noise is independent across features; blood pressure figure is
     visit-to-visit, not same-day. Warren 2010 and Andresen 2003 were confirmed
     from abstracts only.
   - BRFSS noise covers 3 of 21 features; the days-variable categories
     (0, 1-13, 14+) are my assumption.
   - Robustness numbers are not comparable in size between the two datasets.
  Explanations
   - RF TreeSHAP explains probability, the others log-odds.
   - Marginal (interventional) Shapley values evaluate models on unrealistic
     feature combinations when features are correlated.
  Environment
   - Framingham ran on the laptop, BRFSS on Kaggle; torch version differs.
  Literature
   - The survey is not a formal systematic review; no "first" claim is safe
     without a database search.

D-37  Settings fixed by choice were re-checked on BOTH datasets (src/audit4_settings.py).
      Forest size, network width and XGBoost learning rate move explanations about as much
      as a seed. Two things move them more, at unchanged accuracy:
        - MLP optimiser settings (lr 0.001, weight decay 0.0001, dropout 0.1):
          top-3 agreement with the main model 0.74-0.81 Framingham, 0.63-0.79 BRFSS
          (seed-to-seed 0.85-0.90 and 0.90-0.91);
        - a wider search grid: XGBoost on Framingham (depth 1, 200 trees; 0.79 vs seed 0.87)
          and random forest on BRFSS (leaf 25, depth 16; 0.91 vs seed 0.95).
      Consequence: hyperparameter choice is a source of explanation variability that the
      variance decomposition did not include. Reported in paper 4.11, 5.2, 6, Appendix A.
D-38  Analysis settings re-checked from stored explanations (src/audit5_stored.py): number
      of patients, number of bootstraps, k = 1..5, RBO persistence 0.8/0.9/0.95, pairing
      scheme. The bootstrap-below-seed result holds under all of them on both datasets.
      BRFSS day-count banding is not a free choice: kappa = 1 - change rate for any banding.
D-39  Full replication with master seed 7 on both datasets (kaggle/run_replication.py;
      results/replication_seed7/). Every qualitative result reproduced. Not reproduced
      numerically: Framingham test AUC and calibration slope (split-dependent), Framingham
      MLP stability figures (tuning chose 14 epochs instead of 62), Framingham background
      ICC (0.91-0.94 vs 0.95-0.97). Reported in paper 4.14.
Basis of the settings formerly labelled "our choice": common defaults (RF 300 trees, XGB
learning rate 0.05 and subsampling 0.8, MLP 64-32 with dropout 0.2, top-3, RBO 0.9),
compute budget (500 patients, background size, 10 seeds, 50/100 bootstraps, 20 noise draws,
grid sizes), arbitrary (master seed 42). None derived from theory; defended by D-37 to D-39.
