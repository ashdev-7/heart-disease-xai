1. WHAT "VALIDATED" MEANS HERE
================================================================================

Verification = the code computes what the methods section says it computes.
Validation   = the conclusions follow from the evidence and do not depend on
               arbitrary analysis choices.

What this file establishes: both of the above, for the two datasets, four
models and explainers studied, to the extent the listed tests cover.

What no test can establish: that reviewers will accept the paper, that the
findings hold on other clinical data, or that explanations judged plausible by
a risk equation are useful to a clinician. Section 9 lists what is still weak.

================================================================================
2. PROBLEM STATEMENT AND CLAIMS
================================================================================

Problem
  For tabular heart-disease risk models, how much of a patient's feature-
  attribution explanation is determined by the patient, and how much changes
  with model class, explainer, training sample, seed, measurement noise and
  the explainer's background sample?

Claims the paper would make
  C1  Explanations are faithful to the model they explain.
  C2  Grouped importance is plausible against a published risk equation
      (Framingham).
  C3  On small data, a patient's leading features are not reproducible when
      the training data is resampled.
  C4  That irreproducibility is largely a sample-size effect.
  C5  Model class changes an explanation more than explainer choice does.
  C6  SMOTE breaks calibration and changes explanations.
  C7  The explainer's background sample is a non-trivial source of variation.
  C8  Seed-only retraining understates instability.
  C9  A measurement-noise test partly identifies the patients whose
      explanations will not reproduce (small data only; descriptive).

4. VALIDATION (AUDITS 2 AND 3)
================================================================================

4.1 Complete cases (the check promised in Decision D-04)
  56 of 500 explained Framingham patients have an imputed value; 34 have
  imputed glucose. Restricting to the 444 complete cases changes bootstrap
  agreement, noise agreement and plausibility by 0.002 or less, and the
  faithfulness ratio by 0.02. Glucose carries 2-8% of attribution; among
  patients with imputed glucose it carries less for LR and MLP (as expected
  for a value set to the median) and about the same for the tree models.
  Conclusion: imputation does not drive any result.

4.2 Metric choice
  "Bootstrap agreement is lower than seed agreement" holds in 40 of 40
  model x metric cells (top-1, top-3, top-5, Spearman, cosine; both datasets).
  A magnitude-based metric (cosine of raw attribution vectors) stays at
  0.83-0.88 on Framingham while top-3 agreement is 0.52-0.59: magnitude
  metrics hide rank instability, as Hwang et al. 2026 warn. Both are reported.

4.3 Grouping correlated features
  Merging the four blood-pressure, two smoking and two glycaemia columns before
  ranking does not remove the Framingham instability: chance-corrected
  bootstrap agreement is 0.45-0.66 on raw features and 0.45-0.65 on 8 groups.
  The single top GROUP is the same in 70-83% of bootstrap pairs.

4.4 Sign stability (a limit on claim C3)
  For a patient's three leading features, a bootstrap-retrained model gives
  the attribution the same sign 96-100% of the time on Framingham and 100% on
  BRFSS. What is unstable is the ORDER of the leading features, not whether a
  feature raises or lowers risk. The paper must say this; it narrows C3.

4.5 Paired differences with confidence intervals
  Seed-only minus bootstrap agreement, per patient:
      Framingham +0.29 to +0.32 (every CI excludes zero by a wide margin)
      BRFSS      +0.09 to +0.10 for the tree models, +0.02 for the MLP
  Explainer-swap minus model-swap agreement, per patient:
      Framingham +0.27 (95% CI +0.26 to +0.29); +0.20 without the random
      forest; +0.24 for Shapley-type explainers without the random forest
      BRFSS      +0.24 (+0.22 to +0.25); +0.22; +0.22
  C5 and C8 do not rest on the random forest or on Integrated Gradients.

4.6 Threshold choice
  The weak link between bootstrap agreement and distance from the decision
  threshold stays weak at four Framingham thresholds (0.075, 0.10, 0.152,
  0.20) and three BRFSS thresholds; the largest correlation anywhere is 0.45.

4.7 Other train/test splits (answers the single-split weakness, D-01)
  Three further random splits, 30 bootstraps each, 200 patients:
      per-patient top-3      main    split 1  split 2  split 3
      LR  linear SHAP        0.682   0.686    0.719    0.720
      XGB TreeSHAP           0.580   0.616    0.611    0.559
      MLP Expected Grad.     0.574   0.612    0.623    0.581
      RF  TreeSHAP           0.528   0.533    0.524    0.520
      MLP Integr. Grad.      0.515   0.538    0.559    0.509
  Stability results move by at most 0.05 across splits. Logistic regression
  is the most reproducible on every split; the order of the other four rows
  changes between splits (correction 2026-10-05: an earlier version of this
  file said the ordering was unchanged, which the table above contradicts). Base-model test AUC DOES move with the split
  (LR 0.702 on the main split; 0.742, 0.751, 0.736 on the others). The main
  split is the hardest of the four. Performance must be reported as a range.

4.8 Imputation method
  Regression (iterative) imputation instead of the median: each patient's
  explanation agrees with the median-imputation explanation at top-3 0.99
  (LR), 0.97 (MLP-EG), 0.94 (MLP-IG), 0.89 (XGB), 0.83 (RF). For comparison,
  changing only the SEED gives 0.82-0.90, so the imputation method moves
  explanations no more than a seed does. Test AUC unchanged.

4.9 Interventional versus path-dependent TreeSHAP
  Bootstrap agreement is the same under both (RF 0.528 vs 0.545; XGB 0.580 vs
  0.583). The two variants themselves agree at 0.79 (RF) and 0.84 (XGB) on the
  same model, so the choice matters for an individual explanation but not for
  any stability conclusion.

4.10 Resampling scheme, and whether the bootstrap exaggerates
  80% subsampling without replacement gives higher agreement than the
  bootstrap (0.64-0.84 vs 0.52-0.68), as expected, because two 80% subsamples
  share most of their rows.
  Decisive test on BRFSS: 30 bootstraps of ONE sample of n rows versus up to
  30 DISJOINT samples of n rows from the 202,944-row pool.
      n = 3,392 (30 disjoint)     bootstrap   disjoint samples
        LR 0.750 / 0.675; RF 0.615 / 0.616; XGB 0.585 / 0.589;
        MLP-IG 0.635 / 0.601; MLP-EG 0.667 / 0.632
      n = 7,580 (26 disjoint; positives run out after 26)
        LR 0.746 / 0.749; RF 0.702 / 0.625; XGB 0.634 / 0.625;
        MLP-IG 0.634 / 0.667; MLP-EG 0.670 / 0.690
  Differences are between -0.08 and +0.03 with no consistent direction. The
  bootstrap does not exaggerate instability relative to genuinely new samples.

4.11 KernelSHAP budget
  Quadrupling the coalition budget did not reduce KernelSHAP's error. The
  cause was shap's default feature truncation (Section 7.1), not sampling.

4.12 Faithfulness settings
  Ratio of top-k to random-k prediction gap, Framingham:
      k = 1: 3.9-7.2     k = 3: 2.2-3.1     k = 5: 1.7-2.2
  for both replacement rules (random background row; background mean).
  Top-k beats random-k in at least 75% of patients in every setting (the
  minimum is at k = 1 for the random forest).

4.13 Correlated blood-pressure error
  Making systolic and diastolic errors correlated at 0.7 (an assumed value)
  changes noise-robustness scores by 0.007 or less.

4.14 Re-tuning hyperparameters inside every bootstrap (the deviation in D-07)
  Two versions were run on the same 30 Framingham bootstraps, 200 patients.

  (a) Ordinary 5-fold CV inside each bootstrap sample (run on Kaggle, with the
      tuned-once reference recomputed in the same environment)
        model   tuned once   re-tuned   difference (95% CI)
        LR      0.682        0.679      -0.004 (-0.005 to -0.002)
        RF      0.528        0.528       0.000 (same setting chosen 30 of 30)
        XGB     0.585        0.440      -0.145 (-0.160 to -0.129)
      XGBoost chose its most complex setting (600 trees, depth 4) in 30 of 30
      bootstraps, against 100 trees, depth 2 when tuned on the real data.
      Cause: a bootstrap sample contains copies of the same row; ordinary
      K-fold puts copies on both sides of a fold, so the validation fold is
      not independent and complex models are rewarded.

  (b) Leak-free version: grouped folds keep all copies of a row together
        model   tuned once   re-tuned   difference (95% CI)
        LR      0.682        0.692      +0.010 (+0.002 to +0.017)
        XGB     0.580        0.577      -0.003 (-0.005 to -0.002)
        RF      0.528        0.575      +0.047 (+0.032 to +0.062)
      XGBoost now chooses 100 trees, depth 2 in the bootstraps, as on the real
      data. The random forest often chooses a shallower setting (depth 6 in 17
      of 30), which is slightly MORE stable.

  Conclusion: with correct re-tuning, per-patient agreement is the same as
  with hyperparameters tuned once (differences -0.003 to +0.047). Fixing the
  hyperparameters did not hide instability, and the earlier statement that the
  reported instability is "a lower bound" is withdrawn: it is neither clearly
  a lower nor an upper bound. Separately, naive re-tuning inside bootstrap
  samples is itself a pitfall that inflates apparent instability (XGBoost
  0.585 -> 0.440); this is worth one paragraph in the paper.
  Caveat: (b) was run on the laptop and compared with the laptop main run;
  (a) on Kaggle. LR and RF reproduce across the two to three decimals.

5. CLAIMS-EVIDENCE MATRIX
================================================================================

C1 Faithful
   Evidence : top-3 replacement moves log-odds 2.2-3.3x more than random-3;
              97-100% of patients; both datasets.
   Tests    : exact-Shapley ground truth (T2); k = 1, 3, 5; two replacement
              rules; model-randomisation check.
   Limits   : replaced inputs can be unrealistic combinations (Hase et al.
              2021); no retraining-based test (ROAR).
   Status   : SUPPORTED.

C2 Plausible (Framingham)
   Evidence : cosine 0.90-0.96 with Ref-A; random level 0.65-0.74; same top
              three groups for every model and explainer; 1.5-6% of
              attribution on unreferenced features.
   Tests    : bootstrap range; complete cases; KernelSHAP gives the same.
   Limits   : both equation-based references come from one paper; 6 groups;
              the independent reference (INTERHEART) disagrees on lipids.
   Status   : SUPPORTED for "matches a published risk equation". NOT a claim
              of clinical validity.

C3 Not reproducible per patient on small data
   Evidence : Framingham bootstrap top-3 0.52-0.70; 4-47% of patients below
              0.5.
   Tests    : 5 metrics; grouped features; 3 other splits; path-dependent
              TreeSHAP; other imputation; complete cases; bootstrap vs
              disjoint samples; convergence in number of bootstraps.
   Limits   : signs are stable (4.4); one cohort of this size.
              Re-tuning hyperparameters per bootstrap (leak-free) leaves the
              result unchanged (4.14).
   Status   : SUPPORTED, and must be worded as "order of leading features".

C4 Sample-size effect
   Evidence : BRFSS at 15 / 34 / 135 / 910 events per predictor: top-3 rises
              monotonically for LR, XGB, RF and both MLP explainers between 34
              and 910; AUC moves 0.83 -> 0.85.
   Tests    : hyperparameters re-tuned at each size; disjoint-sample check.
   Tests    : also replicated with 2 further subsamples at the two smallest
              sizes and 1 at 30,000 (4.15): means rise at every step.
   Limits   : post-hoc experiment; 2-3 subsamples per size; a single
              subsample can move a cell by about 0.10 at small sizes, and the
              15 vs 34 events-per-predictor ranges overlap.
   Status   : SUPPORTED as exploratory. "Largely" is justified by BRFSS at
              34 events per predictor (0.63-0.75) against Framingham
              (0.52-0.70); the remaining gap is unexplained.

C5 Model class matters more than explainer
   Evidence : model swap 0.51-0.79 / 0.54-0.93; explainer swap 0.79-1.00 /
              0.73-1.00; ICC for model class 0.67 / 0.84 against 0.96 / 1.00
              for explainer (corrected KernelSHAP).
   Tests    : paired difference with CI; without RF; Shapley-type only.
   Limits   : explainers tested are all attribution methods of the Shapley /
              path family. LIME, counterfactuals and rule methods were not
              tested, so the claim is about THESE explainers.
   Status   : SUPPORTED within that scope.

C6 SMOTE
   Evidence : mean predicted risk 0.33-0.43 against 0.152 (Framingham) and
              0.16-0.34 against 0.094 (BRFSS); no AUC gain; top-3 agreement
              with the no-resampling explanation 0.49-0.60 (Framingham
              flexible models).
   Limits   : base models only; hyperparameters not re-tuned for SMOTE; plain
              SMOTE interpolates yes/no features (SMOTE-NC not tried);
              class weighting not compared.
   Status   : SUPPORTED; agrees with van den Goorbergh 2022 and Rai 2025.

C7 Background sample
   Evidence : redrawing 100 background rows changes top-3 membership (0.80-0.93
              agreement); ICC 0.96.
   Tests    : 50 / 100 / 200 rows; 200-row tree lines corrected (7.2).
   Limits   : 100 patients, 5-10 draws.
   Status   : SUPPORTED as "small in magnitude, enough to reorder near-ties".

C8 Seed-only understates
   Evidence : paired differences in 4.5.
   Status   : SUPPORTED on Framingham; small on BRFSS.

C9 Noise test as a flag
   Evidence : Spearman 0.39-0.60 (Framingham), 0.05-0.40 (BRFSS).
   Limits   : many correlations computed; no held-out test of the flag.
   Status   : DESCRIPTIVE ONLY. Should be presented as an observation, not a
              method.

7. ERRORS THE AUDIT FOUND
================================================================================

7.1 KernelSHAP silently kept only 10 features  (Decision D-32)
  shap 0.52's KernelExplainer, by default, fits a regularised regression that
  keeps 10 features and sets the rest to exactly zero. With 15 features, 5 were
  zeroed per patient; with 21, 11 were zeroed.
  Found because KernelSHAP's error against exact values did not shrink with
  budget. Confirmed directly: with the regularisation off, KernelSHAP on LR
  equals exact linear SHAP to 1e-07.
  Fix: l1_reg=False. Framingham re-run locally; BRFSS re-run on Kaggle in the
  same environment as the original BRFSS run (base models refitted there
  reproduce the first run exactly: max difference 0.0).
  Effect on Framingham results:
      a patient's KernelSHAP top-3 before vs after: 0.976-0.999 agreement
      model-swap agreement: 0.515-0.786 before, 0.512-0.788 after
      explainer-swap: LR 0.999 -> 1.000; XGB 0.996 -> 0.998; others +/-0.01
      model-class ICC 0.669 -> 0.668; explainer ICC median 0.960 -> 0.964
      faithfulness and plausibility of KernelSHAP rows: unchanged to 2 decimals
  Effect on BRFSS results (truncation was heavier: 11 of 21 features zeroed):
      KernelSHAP top-3 per patient, before vs after: 0.955-0.991 agreement
      KernelSHAP vs exact linear SHAP on LR: 0.991 -> 1.000 (error 9e-08)
      explainer swap: XGB 0.954 -> 0.997; MLP-EG 0.973 -> 0.979;
                      MLP-IG 0.947 -> 0.951; RF 0.726 -> 0.725
      model swap: 0.54-0.92 before, 0.54-0.93 after
      model-class ICC 0.82 -> 0.84; explainer ICC median 0.96 -> 0.997
      KernelSHAP noise floor: 0.97-1.00 -> 0.99-1.00
      paired explainer-minus-model difference: +0.237 -> +0.240
  The correction makes the explainers agree MORE closely with each other,
  which strengthens claim C5. No conclusion changes. All KernelSHAP tables for
  both datasets were regenerated; superseded tables are marked in the
  base_axes reports and the truncated files are kept as
  kernel_default_truncated.npz.

7.2 Tree explainer capped the background at 100 rows  (D-27)
  Reported earlier. Fixed; affected lines recomputed.

7.3 Random-forest TreeSHAP additivity
  The earlier concern (worst case 5% on Framingham) is narrowed: against exact
  Shapley values the error is 0.28% of the total for the patients tested. The
  rare large-error patients were not individually traced.

7.4 Expectation errors (not code errors), already recorded
  Calibration direction of the risk equation; laptop capacity; glucose
  variation value; two invalid INTERHEART mappings.

8. QUESTIONS A HOSTILE REVIEWER WILL ASK
================================================================================

Q1 "Instability on Framingham is just a bad model. AUC is 0.70."
   The published risk equation scores 0.718 on the same file and PREVENT-type
   equations validate near 0.70; this is the ceiling of the data, not a
   modelling failure. LR, the stablest model, is also the best. And BRFSS at
   AUC 0.83-0.84 with 15-34 events per predictor shows the same instability.

Q2 "The bootstrap overstates instability."
   Tested against disjoint new samples: no (4.10).

Q3 "You fixed hyperparameters, so it is not a full instability analysis."
   Tested (4.14). Re-tuned inside every bootstrap with leak-free folds,
   agreement changes by -0.003 to +0.047. We also show that the common way of
   re-tuning inside bootstrap samples leaks and inflates instability.

Q4 "Top-3 overlap is arbitrary."
   Same conclusions under top-1, top-5, Spearman, RBO; cosine reported too.

Q5 "Correlated features swapping places is trivial instability."
   Grouping them does not remove it (4.3).

Q6 "Your reference is circular: Framingham equation on Framingham data."
   The equation's coefficients come from a different, larger Framingham
   sample (8,491 people) and a different outcome; they were applied untrained.
   Still one source family, which is declared; INTERHEART is the independent
   check and it disagrees on lipids for a stated measurement reason.

Q7 "Plausibility is not correctness."
   Agreed and stated. Faithfulness is tested separately (C1).

Q8 "Model matters more than explainer only because your explainers are all
    Shapley variants."
   Agreed; C5 is scoped to that family. Integrated Gradients, the one
   non-Shapley method, is the largest explainer effect seen (0.81).

Q9 "One split."
   Three more splits give the same stability results (4.7).

Q10 "The sample-size experiment was designed after seeing the data."
   Yes; labelled exploratory. Replicated with further subsamples (4.15) and
   checked against disjoint samples (4.10).

Q11 "Two environments."
   All pinned libraries verified identical; LR and RF reproduce to four
   decimals across them.

Q12 "Is there anything new here?"
   Individual pieces have precedent (Riley & Collins for predictions; Hwang
   et al. for SHAP multiplicity; Salih et al. for model dependence). New, as
   far as the non-systematic survey could tell: (a) decomposition of
   explanation variation by source on cardiovascular tabular data with
   measurement-calibrated noise; (b) the events-per-predictor dose-response
   for explanation stability with a disjoint-sample check; (c) a risk-equation
   Shapley reference; (d) the demonstration that a common SMOTE pipeline
   creates an explanation artefact. No "first" claim should be made without a
   database search.

Q13 "No clinicians, no real clinical deployment."
   True. This is a methods audit on public data and must be titled as one.

================================================================================
9. WEAKNESSES THAT REMAIN
================================================================================

  Serious
   - Two public datasets, one with no codebook, one a prevalence survey.
     No electronic-health-record cohort, no external validation site.
   - No clinician input anywhere.
   - Variance decomposition has no interaction terms.
   - Explainer family is narrow (see Q8).
  Moderate
   - C4 and C7 rest on analyses added after the protocol freeze.
   - Reference built from one paper; only 6 groups.
   - BRFSS noise model covers 3 of 21 features and uses an assumed
     categorisation; BRFSS and Framingham robustness are not comparable.
   - Warren 2010 and Andresen 2003 confirmed from abstracts only.
   - Literature survey is not systematic.
  Minor
   - RF TreeSHAP on probability scale; rare additivity outliers untraced.
   - MLP architecture not tuned.
   - torch version differs between the two environments.

================================================================================
10. WHERE THIS CAN BE PUBLISHED: AN HONEST VIEW
================================================================================

This is my judgement, not something a test can confirm.

  Strengths a reviewer will see: a frozen protocol with a decision register;
  ground-truth verification of every explainer; chance-corrected metrics with
  intervals; a sample-size dose-response with a disjoint-sample check;
  sensitivity analyses for most analysis choices; reproducible code; negative
  findings about the authors' own earlier draft reported openly.

  What caps the venue: public benchmark data only, no clinicians, narrow
  explainer family, findings that extend known results more than they
  overturn anything.

  Realistic targets, in descending ambition:
   - BMC Medical Informatics and Decision Making, Computers in Biology and
     Medicine, Journal of Biomedical Informatics, Artificial Intelligence in
     Medicine: plausible; the last two are a stretch without clinical data.
   - PLOS One, Scientific Reports, PLOS Digital Health: a sound-methods paper
     of this kind fits their criteria well.
   - ML4H or a similar workshop track: good fit for the methods contribution.
  Not realistic without new data or clinician involvement: Lancet Digital
  Health, npj Digital Medicine, Nature Machine Intelligence, NeurIPS/ICML main
  track.

  No one can guarantee acceptance. What can be said is that the specific
  failure modes that sink papers of this type (leakage, uncorrected metrics,
  unverified explainers, single-seed claims, overclaimed novelty) have each
  been tested for and either ruled out or declared.
