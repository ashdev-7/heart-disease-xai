Where does explanation unreliability come from in tabular cardiovascular
risk models?

         justified in Decision_Register.txt. Later changes are logged in
         Section 9 with date and reason.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. QUESTION AND CLAIMS WE WANT TO BE ABLE TO MAKE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Question
  When a cardiovascular risk model explains one patient's prediction, how
  much of the explanation is fixed by the patient, and how much moves with
  (a) the model class, (b) the explanation method, (c) training randomness,
  (d) ordinary measurement noise?

Three sub-questions
  Q1  Sources : what share of attribution variability does each of (a)-(d)
                account for?
  Q2  Linking : per patient, do the reliability axes predict each other, and
                are unreliable explanations concentrated near the decision
                threshold?
  Q3  Plausibility : how far are attributions from published cardiovascular
                risk knowledge, and is that distance caused by the model or
                by the explainer?

Out of scope (decided 2026-10-03 / 04)
  Counterfactuals (DiCE), LIME, EBM, hyperparameter Rashomon sets, clinician
  rankings, a third dataset, new model architectures.

3. PHASE 1 - DATA VALIDATION
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

D1 Provenance and codebook
  Framingham: record the Kaggle source and file hash. Compare summary
    statistics (age range, % male, % smokers, mean sysBP, CHD rate) with
    published descriptions of the cohort. No formal codebook exists on
    Kaggle; this is stated as a limitation.
  BRFSS: map each of the 21 features and the target to the CDC 2015
    codebook variable it came from. Check coding of each against the
    codebook.

D2 Integrity
  Ranges and units per column; impossible values; missingness per column;
  exact-duplicate rows.
  Duplicates in BRFSS are counted but NOT dropped by default: with 21 coarse
  survey answers, two different people can give identical rows. A
  de-duplicated run is a sensitivity analysis.

D3 Known-result replication
  Plain logistic regression, no resampling, train-only preprocessing.
  Expect: age, systolic BP, smoking, diabetes with positive coefficients;
  leakage-free test AUC about 0.70 (Framingham) and 0.81-0.85 (BRFSS), in
  line with published leakage-free results.

D5 Fitness for purpose
  Framingham: prospective 10-year outcome with measured risk factors. Fit
    for all four axes.
  BRFSS: one-time survey; outcome is self-reported existing disease;
    several features (general health, difficulty walking, physical health
    days) are consequences of disease. A prospective prevention equation is
    the wrong yardstick. An association-based reference (case-control odds
    ratios) is the matching design, so BRFSS gets its own, weaker reference
    (Section 4.7). Fit for axes F, R, M; axis P is secondary on BRFSS.

Output: data_validation_report.txt with pass/fail per check.

STOP RULE 1
  If D3 gives a wrong-signed coefficient for age, systolic BP, smoking, or
  diabetes with a confidence interval excluding zero, or an AUC outside
  0.60-0.80 (Framingham) / 0.75-0.90 (BRFSS), stop and investigate the data
  before anything else.

4. PHASE 2 - REFERENCE CONSTRUCTION AND VALIDATION (FRAMINGHAM ONLY)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

4.1 The published equation (retrieved 2026-10-04 from the Framingham Heart
    Study risk-function page; D'Agostino et al. 2008, Circulation 117:743)

  Outcome: general CVD (coronary death, MI, coronary insufficiency, angina,
  ischaemic and haemorrhagic stroke, TIA, peripheral artery disease, heart
  failure). Population: ages 30-74, free of CVD at baseline.
  Age, cholesterol, HDL, systolic BP, BMI enter as natural logs.

  BMI-based (office) model            Men        Women
    ln age                            3.11296    2.72107
    ln BMI                            0.79277    0.51125
    ln SBP, untreated                 1.85508    2.81291
    ln SBP, treated                   1.92672    2.88267
    smoking                           0.70953    0.61868
    diabetes                          0.53160    0.77763
    baseline survival S0(10)          0.88431    0.94833

  Lipid-based model                   Men        Women
    ln age                            3.06117    2.32888
    ln total cholesterol              1.12370    1.20904
    ln HDL cholesterol               -0.93263   -0.70833
    ln SBP, untreated                 1.93303    2.76157
    ln SBP, treated                   1.99881    2.82263
    smoking                           0.65451    0.52873
    diabetes                          0.57367    0.69154
    baseline survival S0(10)          0.88936    0.95012

  Mapping to the Kaggle file: age, male, BMI, sysBP, BPMeds (treated),
  currentSmoker, diabetes. All present. HDL is absent, so the lipid model
  cannot be computed, only its coefficients used.

4.2 D4 - Does the equation work on this data?
  Apply the BMI-based model to patients aged 30-74 with prevalentStroke = 0
  (the only prior-CVD flag in the file).
  Report: AUC with bootstrap CI against TenYearCHD; observed vs predicted
  by decile.
  Expected: AUC about 0.65-0.75. Predicted risk should exceed the observed
  CHD rate, because the equation predicts all CVD and the target is CHD
  only. So discrimination is the acceptance test, not calibration.

  STOP RULE 2
    If AUC < 0.60, the data and the reference do not fit each other. Stop
    and reconsider the reference before building anything on it.

4.3 Feature groups (applied to model attributions before any comparison)

  Age            age
  Sex            male
  Blood pressure sysBP, diaBP, prevalentHyp, BPMeds
  Smoking        currentSmoker, cigsPerDay
  Glycaemia      diabetes, glucose
  Adiposity      BMI
  Lipids         totChol
  Unreferenced   education, heartRate, prevalentStroke

  Group attribution = sum of member attributions (signed), then absolute
  value. Unreferenced features are excluded from rank comparisons and
  reported separately as "share of attribution mass on unreferenced
  features".

4.4 R1 - Three references

  Ref-A (primary)  Exact Shapley values of the BMI-based equation computed
                   on the Framingham explained set, sex included as a
                   feature (7 inputs, all coalitions enumerated). Gives a
                   per-patient and a global importance per group.
                   Covers: Age, Sex, Blood pressure, Smoking, Glycaemia,
                   Adiposity. Not Lipids.
  Ref-B            Lipid-model coefficients times the standard deviation of
                   each (log) variable in our data, sex-averaged.
                   Covers: Age, Blood pressure, Smoking, Glycaemia, Lipids.
                   HDL absent in data. Global only.
  Ref-C            INTERHEART 2004 ordering of modifiable factors by odds
                   ratio (values in Section 4.7). Covers Smoking, Lipids,
                   Blood pressure, Glycaemia, Adiposity; age and sex are not
                   in it (cases and controls were matched on them). Ordinal
                   only.

4.5 R2 - Ceiling
  Agreement among Ref-A, Ref-B, Ref-C on the groups they share (Kendall
  tau-b). No model can be expected to agree with "clinical knowledge" more
  than clinical sources agree with each other.

4.6 Known weaknesses, stated up front
  - Reference outcome is general CVD; target is CHD.
  - Only 6-7 groups, so rank correlations have wide intervals. Therefore the
    primary plausibility metric uses magnitudes (Section 6, Axis P).
  - Ref-A is itself an explanation of a model. It is like-for-like, which is
    the point, but it inherits the choice of background distribution.

4.7 Phase 2B - BRFSS reference (secondary)

  Why a different kind of reference
    BRFSS records who already has heart disease. The matching evidence is
    case-control association with myocardial infarction, not a 10-year risk
    equation. INTERHEART (Yusuf et al. 2004, Lancet 364:937-952; 15,152
    cases, 14,820 controls, 52 countries) is that design.

  INTERHEART values. VERIFIED 2026-10-04 against the paper itself (Table 3,
  fully adjusted column "OR 2", 99% confidence intervals). Analysed sample:
  12,461 cases and 14,637 controls (15,152 and 14,820 enrolled).

    Factor                Comparison               OR (99% CI)        PAR    BRFSS item
    ApoB/ApoA1 ratio      top vs lowest quintile   3.25 (2.81-3.76)   49.2%  HighChol*
    Psychosocial index    any vs none              2.67 (2.21-3.22)   32.5%  MentHlth*
    Diabetes              history vs none          2.37 (2.07-2.71)    9.9%  Diabetes
    Smoking               current+former vs never  2.04 (1.86-2.25)   35.7%  Smoker
      (current vs never: 2.87 (2.58-3.19))
    Hypertension          history vs none          1.91 (1.74-2.10)   17.9%  HighBP
    Abdominal obesity     top vs lowest tertile    1.62 (1.45-1.80)   20.1%  (none)
    Fruit and veg daily   yes vs no                0.70 (0.62-0.79)   13.7%  Fruits, Veggies
    Exercise              >= 4 h/week vs less      0.86 (0.76-0.97)   12.2%  PhysActivity*
    Alcohol               >= 3 times/week vs less  0.91 (0.82-1.02)    6.7%  (none)
    (* = imperfect proxy; reported with and without these items)

  Facts confirmed from the paper that shape the mapping
    - Hypertension and diabetes are SELF-REPORTED history. Measured blood
      pressure was recorded but deliberately not used. This matches BRFSS
      HighBP and Diabetes directly.
    - BRFSS "Smoker" (ever smoked 100 cigarettes) matches current+former vs
      never, so 2.04 is the right figure, not 2.87.
    - BMI is NOT a valid stand-in for abdominal obesity: the paper states
      BMI was a weaker predictor than waist/hip ratio and became
      non-significant once waist/hip ratio was in the model. No BMI odds
      ratio is reported. BMI is therefore unreferenced in Ref-D.
    - Alcohol in INTERHEART is regular drinking (protective, p = 0.03).
      BRFSS HvyAlcoholConsump is heavy drinking, a different exposure.
      Not mapped.
    - Exercise is >= 4 hours a week of moderate or strenuous activity;
      BRFSS PhysActivity is any activity in the past 30 days. Loose proxy.
    - Cases and controls were matched on age and sex, so the study gives no
      importance for Age or Sex.
    - Effects of hypertension and diabetes were larger in women than men.
    - The abstract of the paper swaps the two abdominal-obesity odds ratios;
      Table 3 is the correct one (top vs lowest tertile = 1.62).

  Ref-D therefore covers 7 BRFSS items directly or by proxy: HighChol*,
  MentHlth*, Diabetes, Smoker, HighBP, Fruits/Veggies, PhysActivity*.

  Ref-D  Rank BRFSS items by |ln OR| from INTERHEART. Covers 7 items
         (4 exact, 3 proxies). Age, Sex, BMI and alcohol are not covered.
  Ref-E  Ordering of Age, Sex, HighBP, HighChol, Smoker, Diabetes, BMI
         implied by the D'Agostino equations. Ordinal only, because BRFSS
         records these as yes/no or bands, not measured values.

  BRFSS feature classes
    Referenced      HighBP, HighChol, Smoker, Diabetes, Fruits, Veggies,
                    PhysActivity, MentHlth (Ref-D); Age, Sex, BMI (Ref-E only)
    Unreferenced    HvyAlcoholConsump
    Downstream      GenHlth, PhysHlth, DiffWalk
    Prior disease   Stroke
    Access / social Income, Education, AnyHealthcare, NoDocbcCost, CholCheck

  What is reported for BRFSS
    Primary  : share of attribution mass in each class. How much of the
               explanation rests on consequences of disease is the
               informative number here.
    Secondary: rank agreement with Ref-D and Ref-E on referenced items.

  Remaining weakness: people diagnosed with heart disease change behaviour
  (quit smoking, change diet), so survey associations can be weaker or
  reversed relative to INTERHEART. Low agreement on BRFSS is therefore not
  evidence against a model or explainer, and is not used for any verdict.

5. PHASES 3 AND 4 - MODELS AND EXPLANATIONS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

5.1 Splits
  One stratified 80/20 split per dataset, fixed. The test set is never used
  for fitting, tuning, imputation, or scaling.
  Explained set: 500 test patients per dataset, stratified by outcome,
  fixed.

5.2 Training variants (per model, per dataset)
  base       1   seed 42, full training set
  seed      10   seeds 0-9, full training set (RF, XGBoost, MLP)
  bootstrap 100 (Framingham) / 50 (BRFSS)
                 resample training rows with replacement, model seed fixed;
                 preprocessing refitted in every variant
                 (Decision Register D-05, D-06)
  Hyperparameters are tuned once on the base training set by 5-fold CV and
  then held fixed for all variants.

5.3 Models
  LR (L2), RF, XGBoost, MLP (same 64-32 architecture as the draft).
  Imbalance: no resampling, decision threshold tuned on a validation split
  (primary). SMOTE-in-pipeline as a sensitivity arm on base models only.
  Reported per model: AUC with CI, Brier score, calibration slope.

5.4 Explainers
  Native (cheap; used on every training variant)
    LR   exact linear SHAP (interventional)
    RF   TreeSHAP, interventional, fixed background
    XGB  TreeSHAP, interventional, fixed background
    MLP  Expected Gradients; Integrated Gradients with mean baseline
  Common (expensive; base models only)
    KernelSHAP on all four models, same 100-row background from real
    training data, fixed sample budget, fixed seed.

  What each comparison isolates
    same explainer (KernelSHAP), different model  -> model effect
    same model, KernelSHAP vs native              -> explainer effect
      on LR this is a ground-truth check of KernelSHAP settings
      on RF/XGB both target the same quantity, so the gap is estimator error
      on MLP the methods target different quantities
    same model and explainer, different variant   -> training effect
    same everything, noised input                 -> measurement effect
    same everything, explainer re-run             -> noise floor

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
6. PHASE 5 - THE FOUR AXES
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Every score is reported with: a bootstrap confidence interval, a null
(random attribution / random ranking), and where relevant the noise floor
(explainer re-run with nothing changed).

Axis F - Faithfulness
  Prediction gap when the top-3 attributed features are replaced by
  background values, versus when the bottom-3 are replaced. A faithful
  explanation gives a large gap for top and a small gap for bottom.
  Null: random choice of 3 features.
  Ground-truth check: on LR, exact linear SHAP is the truth.

Axis R - Robustness to measurement noise
  Framingham: noise per continuous feature from published within-person
    variation. 20 draws per patient. Two levels (low, high) are run because
    sources differ.

    Feature     Low     High    Source                              Status
    sysBP       4.3%    8.6%    Warren et al. 2010, Br J Gen Pract   abstract
    diaBP       4.3%    8.6%    (office within-person CV 8.6% for    abstract
                                both; PMC2930221)
    totChol     3.0%    5.95%   Ricos database, Westgard 2014        opened
    glucose     2.8%    5.6%    Ricos database, Westgard 2014;       opened
                                5.7% in Chai et al. 2017
    BMI         held fixed      only source found is a one-person study
                                (Schneditz et al. 2023); not used
    heartRate   1.5     3.0 bpm Quer et al. 2020, PLOS One           opened
                                (wrist wearable, 92,457 people;
                                not a clinic pulse reading)
    cigsPerDay  held fixed      no source found
    age, binary and ordinal items held fixed

    High = the published within-person figure. Low = half of it, as a
    sensitivity level (a choice, not a published value).
    Status: "opened" = I read the page; "abstract" = figure confirmed from
    the abstract/record, full text not read.
    All levels are relative to the patient's own value, except heart rate.
    Correction 2026-10-04: an earlier note gave glucose as 4.5%; the Ricos
    value is 5.6%.
    The Warren BP figure is variation between office visits. It is larger
    than variation between readings in one visit, so "a re-measurement the
    same day" is NOT what the high level represents; it represents a
    re-measurement at another visit.

  BRFSS: noise calibrated to published test-retest reliability. For each
    item, the flip rate is chosen so that a simulated re-interview
    reproduces the published kappa.
      GenHlth        kappa 0.75   (Andresen et al. 2003, J Epidemiol
                                   Community Health; 868 Missouri adults
                                   re-interviewed after about 2 weeks;
                                   PMC1732444; via Pierannunzi 2013)
      PhysHlth       kappa 0.71
      MentHlth       kappa 0.67
      PhysActivity   kappa 0.67-0.86
    Items with no published item-level reliability (HighBP, HighChol,
    Diabetes, Smoker, BMI, others) are held fixed. Consequence: BRFSS
    robustness is measured against noise in 4 of 21 features only, and
    cannot be compared in size with Framingham robustness.
  Draws that change the predicted class are analysed separately.
  Metrics: top-3 Jaccard, top-5 Jaccard, Kendall tau-b on feature ranks
  between clean and noised attributions.

Axis M - Multiplicity across retraining
  For seeds and bootstraps separately:
    per patient : mean pairwise top-3 Jaccard and rank correlation of that
                  patient's attributions across training variants
    global      : rank correlation of mean |attribution| rankings
    alongside   : spread of the patient's predicted risk across variants
                  (prediction instability)

Axis P - Plausibility (Framingham only)
  Primary  : cosine similarity between the normalised group-importance
             vector of the model explanation and of each reference.
  Secondary: Kendall tau-b on group ranks; share of attribution mass on
             unreferenced features.
  Context  : null from random permutations; ceiling from Section 4.5.

7. PHASE 6 - ANALYSIS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

A1 Variance decomposition (Q1)
  Attributions normalised per explanation to sum to 1 in absolute value.
  For each dataset, a variance-components model per feature group with
  factors: model class, explainer, training variant, noise draw, patient.
  Report the share of variance per factor.

A2 Linking (Q2)
  Per patient: Spearman correlation between the F, R, M scores, and between
  each score and distance of predicted risk from the decision threshold.

A3 Model versus explainer (Q3)
  Plausibility with explainer held fixed across models, and with model held
  fixed across explainers. Compare the two ranges.

Planned outputs
  Table 1  data validation results
  Table 2  reference importance and between-reference agreement
  Table 3  model performance with CI and calibration
  Table 4  axis scores per model x explainer, with CI, null, floor
  Figure 1 variance shares per dataset
  Figure 2 per-patient reliability versus distance to threshold
  Figure 3 plausibility: model effect versus explainer effect
