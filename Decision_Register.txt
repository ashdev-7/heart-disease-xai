================================================================================
DECISION REGISTER
Every methodological choice, the alternatives, the evidence, and its status
================================================================================
Started 2026-10-04. Rule (set by the user 2026-10-04): no choice goes into the
pipeline unless it is checked against prior work and can be defended.

Evidence tags
  [V] opened/confirmed online in this project   [A] abstract confirmed only
  [K] standard reference, from background knowledge, not re-opened
  [E] our own empirical check (file in results/ named)
Where the literature does not settle a choice, that is said, and the choice is
backed by an empirical check or flagged as a limitation.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
A. DATA AND DESIGN
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

D-01  One fixed stratified 80/20 split; test set never used for fitting
  Alternatives: cross-validation; bootstrap optimism correction with no split.
  Evidence: Steyerberg & Harrell 2016 (J Clin Epidemiol) [A] call random
    split-sample validation inefficient and unstable compared with bootstrap
    for ESTIMATING PERFORMANCE. That criticism applies here and is accepted.
  Why we still split: the study compares explanations for the SAME patients
    across hundreds of models. That needs a fixed set of patients that no
    model was trained on. Performance is not the estimand.
  Mitigation: performance is reported with bootstrap confidence intervals and
    across all training variants, never from one model alone.
  Status: defensible for the stated purpose; split-sample weakness declared.

D-02  Explained set = 500 test patients, stratified by outcome, fixed
  Evidence: no standard exists. OpenXAI and Qureshi et al. 2026 [V] evaluate
    on test subsets of similar size; the draft used 30 for stability.
  Empirical backing: bootstrap confidence intervals over patients are
    reported for every score, so the adequacy of 500 is visible in the width
    of the intervals. [E, Phase 5]
  Status: defensible; precision shown rather than assumed.

D-03  No resampling for class imbalance (primary); SMOTE as sensitivity arm
  Alternatives: SMOTE (the draft), class weights, under-sampling.
  Evidence: van den Goorbergh et al. 2022 (JAMIA) [V]: random over/under-
    sampling and SMOTE give miscalibrated models that overestimate risk, with
    no gain in discrimination. Rai et al. 2025 (Front Artif Intell) [V]:
    imbalance handling changes LIME and SHAP rankings.
  Empirical backing: plain LR with no resampling reached test AUC 0.702 on
    Framingham, at least equal to the draft's SMOTE LR (0.698).
    [E, results/data_validation_report.txt]
  Status: supported by literature and by our data.

D-04  Missing values: single imputation, train-only (median; mode for binary)
  Alternatives: multiple imputation; missing indicators; native NaN handling
    in XGBoost.
  Evidence: Sperrin et al. 2020 (J Clin Epidemiol 125:183-187) [A] argue that
    for PREDICTION, missing data handling must be something that can be
    applied at prediction time, which multiple imputation using the outcome
    cannot. Sisk et al. 2023 (Stat Methods Med Res) [A] study regression
    imputation and missing indicators as deployable options.
  Why this choice: all four models must see identical inputs for a patient,
    so explanations differ only because of the model. Native NaN handling in
    XGBoost alone would break that.
  Limitation: glucose is 9.2% missing in Framingham; its attribution is
    biased toward zero for imputed patients. Sensitivity: scores recomputed
    on patients with complete data. [E, Phase 5]
  Status: defensible; simpler than regression imputation, declared.

D-05  Preprocessing refitted inside every training variant
  Evidence: Riley & Collins 2023 (Biometrical J) [V]: instability must be
    assessed by repeating ALL model-building steps in each bootstrap sample.
  Status: follows the cited method.

D-06  Training variants: bootstrap B = 100 (Framingham), 50 (BRFSS);
      10 seeds each for RF, XGBoost, MLP
  Evidence: Riley & Collins 2023 [V] illustrate with large B (e.g. 1,000) for
    instability PLOTS of individual predictions. Bouthillier et al. 2021
    (MLSys) [A]: data sampling, initialisation and hyperparameters are
    separate sources of variance and should each be varied. No source gives
    a required B for mean pairwise agreement of explanations.
  Constraint [E, src/bench_timing.py, this laptop]: one variant of all four
    models with native explanations takes about 40 s (Framingham) and 110 s
    (BRFSS); random-forest TreeSHAP dominates. B = 100 / 50 is about 3 hours.
  Empirical backing required: every multiplicity score is recomputed on the
    first half of the bootstraps; if half-B and full-B differ by more than
    the confidence interval, B is increased. [E, Phase 5]
  Deviation from Riley & Collins: smaller B than their examples. Declared.
  Status: defensible conditional on the convergence check.

D-07  Hyperparameters tuned once (5-fold CV on base training set, log loss),
      then fixed for all variants
  Evidence: Riley & Collins 2023 [V] would re-tune in every bootstrap.
    Van Calster et al. 2019 (BMC Med) [A] stress calibration, which supports
    tuning on a proper scoring rule (log loss) rather than AUC.
  Why fixed: hyperparameter-induced multiplicity was removed from scope on
    2026-10-03 to fit the time available. Fixing them isolates data
    resampling and seed effects.
  Consequence: reported instability is a LOWER BOUND on real-world
    instability. Declared as a limitation.
  Status: deviation, declared.

D-08  Sample size relative to model flexibility is reported, not assumed
  Evidence: van der Ploeg, Austin & Steyerberg 2014 (BMC Med Res Methodol)
    [V]: random forests, neural networks and SVMs may need over 10 times as
    many events per variable as logistic regression for stable AUC and small
    optimism. Riley et al. 2020 (BMJ) [A]: sample size criteria for
    prediction model development.
  Our numbers: Framingham training set has about 515 events for 15
    predictors (about 34 events per predictor): adequate for LR, well short
    of what flexible models need. BRFSS: about 19,100 events for 21
    predictors (about 900 per predictor).
  Consequence: on Framingham, instability of RF/XGBoost/MLP explanations is
    EXPECTED from sample size alone. The paper must say so and must not
    present it as a defect of the explanation method.
  Status: literature-grounded expectation, to be tested by the data.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
B. MODELS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

D-09  Model set: logistic regression, random forest, XGBoost, MLP
  Evidence: Christodoulou et al. 2019 (J Clin Epidemiol) [K]: LR is the
    reference standard in clinical prediction. Grinsztajn et al. 2022
    (NeurIPS) [K]: tree ensembles are the strongest family on tabular data.
    Section 13.3 of Literature_Survey.txt [V/S]: gradient boosting is the
    most used family in heart-disease XAI papers. MLP kept for continuity
    with the draft and because gradient-based explainers require it.
  Status: supported.

D-10  MLP: 64-32 ReLU, dropout 0.2, Adam; epochs chosen once by early
      stopping on a 15% validation split of the base training set, then the
      same epoch count for every variant with no validation split
  Evidence: architecture carried over from the draft; no claim of optimality
    (Grinsztajn 2022 [K] and Shwartz-Ziv & Armon 2022 [K] show MLPs are
    rarely best on tabular data).
  Why fixed epochs: in a bootstrap sample duplicated rows would fall on both
    sides of a train/validation split and make early stopping optimistic.
    Fixing the epoch count avoids that and keeps training size equal across
    variants.
  Status: defensible; own reasoning on the bootstrap leak, no citation found.

D-11  Random forest depth is capped (max_depth <= 12) and leaves >= 10
  Reason: interventional TreeSHAP cost grows with leaves x trees x background
    rows; unrestricted forests on 203k rows are not explainable in the time
    available. [E, bench_timing.py: 69 s for 500 patients at depth 12]
  Evidence: Probst, Wright & Boulesteix 2019 (WIREs DMKD) [K]: random forest
    performance is comparatively insensitive to tuning.
  Check: capped forest's test AUC is compared with the plain-LR AUC; a
    clear loss would reopen this. [E, Phase 3]
  Status: compute-driven constraint, declared, checked.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
C. EXPLANATIONS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

D-12  Explain the log-odds margin; random forest TreeSHAP explains probability
  Evidence: Lundberg et al. 2020 (Nat Mach Intell) [K]: TreeSHAP is exact on
    the scale where the model is additive over trees. For boosted trees that
    is the margin; for a random forest it is the averaged probability.
  Consequence: RF TreeSHAP values are on a different scale from the others.
    All cross-model comparisons therefore use (a) ranks, (b) attributions
    normalised to sum to 1 in absolute value, and (c) the KernelSHAP arm,
    which explains the log-odds of all four models on one scale.
  Status: follows the method's own exactness condition; mismatch handled.

D-13  Interventional (marginal) Shapley values with real training rows as
      background, not conditional values and not the SMOTE distribution
  Evidence: Janzing et al. 2020 (AISTATS) [K] and Chen et al. 2020 [V]:
    interventional values are "true to the model", which is the question
    here (what did THIS model use). Aas et al. 2021 [V] show conditional
    values are preferable when the aim is to be true to the data, and that
    marginal values evaluate the model on unrealistic combinations when
    features are correlated.
  Limitation: with correlated features (four blood-pressure columns) the
    interventional choice splits credit among them. Handled by grouping
    (protocol 4.3), following Aas et al.'s grouped-Shapley proposal.
  Status: supported; limitation handled.

D-14  Background set: 100 real training rows, fixed; sensitivity to size
  Evidence: Yuan et al. 2022 (arXiv 2204.11351) [A]: on MIMIC-III, SHAP
    values and rankings fluctuate with the random background sample, the
    fluctuation falls as background size grows, and top and bottom ranks are
    more stable than middle ranks.
  Empirical backing required: base-model explanations are recomputed with
    several different background draws and with 50 / 100 / 200 rows; the
    spread is reported as part of the explainer noise floor. [E, Phase 5]
  Status: literature says the choice matters, so it is measured.

D-15  Integrated Gradients: mean-of-background baseline, 256 steps;
      Expected Gradients: every background row as baseline, 16 steps each
  Evidence: Sundararajan et al. 2017 (ICML) [K]: IG with completeness check;
    Sturmfels et al. 2020 [K] and Haug et al. 2021 [V]: baseline choice
    changes attributions on tabular data and the zero vector is a poor
    default. Erion et al. 2021 (Nat Mach Intell) [K]: Expected Gradients
    removes the single-baseline choice by averaging over the data.
  Check [E, Framingham base MLP]: IG completeness error (max over 500
    patients) is 0.011 at 64 steps, 0.0035 at 256, 0.0006 at 1,024. 256 steps
    chosen: error about 0.4% of the mean attribution total. Expected
    Gradients (100 baselines x 16 steps): max error 0.005, about 0.7%.
  Status: supported; verified numerically.

D-16  Own implementations for linear SHAP, IG and Expected Gradients
  Reason: each is a few lines and can be verified exactly (additivity /
    completeness), which avoids version-specific library behaviour.
  Check [E, results/framingham/explainer_checks.txt]: additivity error is
    5e-07 for linear SHAP and 2e-06 for XGBoost TreeSHAP.
  Known imperfection: random-forest interventional TreeSHAP (shap 0.52) has
    mean additivity error 0.0006 and maximum 0.0095 on the probability scale,
    against a mean attribution total of 0.18 (0.3% on average, 5% worst
    case). Same result with 32- and 64-bit inputs. Cause not established.
    It is reported, and RF results are also available through KernelSHAP.
  Status: defensible because verified, not because of authority.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
D. METRICS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

D-17  Agreement metrics: top-k overlap (k = 3, 5), rank correlation, and
      rank-biased overlap; ALWAYS reported against a chance baseline
  Evidence: Krishna et al. 2022 [V] define feature agreement and rank
    agreement for explanations. Nogueira, Sechidis & Brown 2018 (JMLR
    18(174)) [A]: a stability measure should be corrected for chance, among
    five required properties; raw overlap measures are not. Webber, Moffat &
    Zobel 2010 (ACM TOIS) [A]: rank-biased overlap weights top ranks more
    and handles the tail gracefully. Hwang et al. 2026 [V]: magnitude-based
    metrics can hide rank instability; use randomised null baselines.
  Choice: report raw top-k Jaccard (comparable with Krishna and the draft)
    together with its expected value under random rankings, and a
    chance-corrected version (observed - expected) / (1 - expected).
  Status: supported.

D-18  Performance: AUC with bootstrap CI, Brier score, calibration slope and
      calibration-in-the-large
  Evidence: Van Calster et al. 2019 (BMC Med) [A]; TRIPOD+AI, Collins et al.
    2024 (BMJ) [V].
  Status: supported.

D-19  Global importance = mean absolute attribution over patients
  Evidence: Lundberg et al. 2020 [K]. Phase 2 showed this is a POPULATION
    measure, to be compared with attributable risk, not with per-person
    odds ratios. [E, results/reference_validation_report.txt]
  Status: supported; interpretation fixed by our own check.

D-20  Decision threshold for "near the threshold" analysis
  Framingham: 20% ten-year risk. Evidence: the high-risk category used with
    Framingham risk scores (D'Agostino 2008 [A]; ATP III [K]).
  BRFSS: no clinical threshold exists for a prevalence model; the outcome
    prevalence is used and labelled as a convention.
  Sensitivity: prevalence threshold also run on Framingham.
  Status: Framingham supported; BRFSS is a convention, declared.

E. LATER PHASES (registered 2026-10-04, before those phases were run)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

D-21  KernelSHAP: shap default budget (2 x features + 2,048 coalitions), same
      100 background rows, logit link, fixed seed; re-run 5 times for the
      noise floor
  Evidence: Covert & Lee 2021 (AISTATS) [V]: KernelSHAP is a stochastic
    estimator; convergence should be checked and uncertainty reported;
    paired sampling reduces variance. The shap default budget is the
    documented setting [V, shap documentation].
  Check: on the LR model the exact answer is known (linear SHAP). The gap
    between KernelSHAP and exact linear SHAP is reported as the estimator
    error at this budget. [E, Phase 4]
  Status: supported; error measured against a known truth.

D-22  Faithfulness: prediction gap when the top-k features are replaced versus
      when k random features are replaced, k = 3; replacement values drawn
      from the background rows (marginal), not zeros or means
  Evidence: Agarwal et al. 2022 (OpenXAI) [V]: PGI / PGU prediction-gap
    metrics. Hase, Xie & Bansal 2021 (NeurIPS) [A]: removing features creates
    out-of-distribution inputs, and the replacement function matters.
    Hooker et al. 2019 (ROAR) [K]: same concern, solved there by retraining.
  Why marginal replacement: it is the same operation that defines the
    interventional Shapley values being tested (D-13), so method and test
    are consistent. Limitation: still out-of-distribution for correlated
    features. Retraining (ROAR) is out of scope.
  Status: supported with a declared limitation.

D-23  Noise model: independent, zero-mean Gaussian error on each continuous
      measurement, multiplicative for quantities with a published CV,
      additive for heart rate; 20 draws per patient; two levels (protocol 6)
  Evidence: classical measurement-error model (Carroll et al., Measurement
    Error in Nonlinear Models) [K]. Magnitudes from Warren 2010 [A], Ricos
    database [V], Quer 2020 [V].
  Limitation: errors in systolic and diastolic pressure are correlated in
    reality; independence overstates the spread of their difference. Stated.
  Status: standard model; simplification declared.

D-24  Variance decomposition framed as a generalizability study: patient is
      the object of measurement; model, explainer, training sample and noise
      draw are facets; report variance components and intraclass
      correlations (reliability of one explanation for one patient)
  Evidence: Shavelson & Webb, generalizability theory [K]; Shrout & Fleiss
    1979 (Psychol Bull) [A] for ICC forms; Koo & Li 2016 (J Chiropr Med) [A]
    for reporting and the conventional bands (<0.5 poor, 0.5-0.75 moderate,
    0.75-0.9 good, >0.9 excellent). Bouthillier et al. 2021 [A] for treating
    data sampling and initialisation as separate variance sources.
  Novelty note: a search found no prior use of generalizability theory or
    ICCs for feature-attribution reliability; closest is RoSHAP (arXiv
    2605.15154, distribution of attributions across resamples) [S]. Not a
    systematic search, so no "first" claim.
  Caution: Koo & Li bands are conventions from rater studies, not validated
    thresholds for explanations. Reported as context, not as verdicts.
  Status: established statistical method, new application; declared.

D-25  Rank-biased overlap persistence p = 0.9
  Evidence: Webber, Moffat & Zobel 2010 [A]: p sets how top-heavy the measure
    is; p = 0.9 puts about 86% of the weight on the first 10 ranks, a common
    default. With 15-21 features this weights the top 3-5 most.
  Status: conventional; chance level computed for our feature counts.

D-26  Two computing environments: Framingham on the laptop, BRFSS on Kaggle
  Reason: the laptop has 7.6 GB RAM and BRFSS jobs were stopped twice for
    low memory. All BRFSS numbers (base models, 60 variants, all base-model
    tests) come from one Kaggle CPU run with the tuned hyperparameters
    carried over. The laptop's BRFSS base models and its single variant
    were discarded (kept in results/brfss_local_discarded, not used).
  Reproducibility check [E]: laptop and Kaggle base models agree on test
    AUC to four decimals for LR (0.8468) and RF (0.8481); XGBoost differs
    by 0.0002 (0.8511 vs 0.8509) and MLP by 0.0001. Hyperparameters and
    seeds were identical.
  Verified 2026-10-04 from the Kaggle run log (retrieved through the API):
    python 3.13.15, scikit-learn 1.8.0, xgboost 3.1.3, shap 0.52.0,
    numpy 2.2.6, torch 2.11.0+cpu. Laptop: python 3.13.7, same
    scikit-learn, xgboost, shap and numpy; torch 2.9.1+cpu.
    So every pinned library matched. torch was not pinned and differs,
    which accounts for the 0.0001 AUC difference of the MLP. The XGBoost
    difference of 0.0002 occurs with identical versions and is attributed
    to hardware / thread count (4 cores vs 12); not investigated further.
  Status: verified.

D-27  Correction to the background-sensitivity test (D-14)
  Verified 2026-10-04 [E]: given a plain 200-row array, shap's tree
  explainer keeps 100 rows (explainer.data has shape (100, F)) and warns;
  given shap.maskers.Independent(data, max_samples=200) it keeps all 200 and
  its expected value equals the mean model output over the 200 rows.
  Fix: src/common.py now always passes the masker with max_samples = number
  of background rows. Results with the standard 100-row background are
  unaffected. The 200-row tree lines were recomputed
  (src/fix_background_200.py) and appended to both base_axes reports:
      Framingham  XGB 0.947, RF 0.827     BRFSS  XGB 0.778, RF 0.870
  (top-3 agreement with the fixed 100-row explanation; 100 patients).
  Status: verified and corrected.

D-28  Sample-size experiment on BRFSS (added after seeing the results)
  Question: is the Framingham-BRFSS gap in bootstrap stability a
    sample-size effect?
  Design: fixed stratified subsamples of the BRFSS training set of size
    3,392 (Framingham's n; about 15 events per predictor), 7,580 (about 34
    events per predictor, Framingham's ratio) and 30,000 (about 135);
    full size already run (about 900). At each size: hyperparameters
    re-tuned by the same 5-fold procedure, 30 bootstraps of the subsample,
    native explainers, 200 explained patients, same background rows.
  Evidence: van der Ploeg et al. 2014 [V] studied stability as a function
    of events per variable by subsampling; Riley & Collins 2023 [V] tie
    instability to development sample size. Matching on events per
    predictor as well as on n follows Riley et al. 2020 [A].
  B = 30 is justified empirically: half-sample checks changed the scores
    by 0.02 or less on Framingham and 0.003 on BRFSS. [E]
  Limits: one subsample per size; this is a post-hoc experiment, labelled
    exploratory in the paper.
  Status: exploratory, declared.

D-29  SMOTE arm: base models only, SMOTE (k = 5) on the preprocessed training
      set, same hyperparameters as the no-resampling models
  Evidence: Chawla et al. 2002 [K]; van den Goorbergh et al. 2022 [V]
    (miscalibration); Rai et al. 2025 [V] (explanations change).
  Not re-tuned, so the comparison isolates the resampling step. Declared.
  Reported: calibration, per-patient top-3 agreement with the no-resampling
    explanation, and plausibility (Framingham).
  Status: supported.

D-30  Variance decomposition as run (amends protocol 7, A1)
  One facet at a time, not a fully crossed design: for each source (model
    class, explainer, bootstrap, seed, measurement noise, background draw)
    the other sources are held at their base setting. Per feature, a
    one-way random-effects ANOVA over patients gives between-patient and
    within-patient (facet) variance of the normalised attribution share;
    components are summed over features. Reported: ICC(1,1) =
    between / (between + within), i.e. the reliability of a single
    explanation with respect to that source (Shrout & Fleiss 1979 [A]).
  Background draw is added as a facet (found to matter; Yuan et al. 2022
    [A]). Interactions between sources are NOT estimated. Declared.
  Status: established method, simplified design, declared.

D-31  Linking analysis: Spearman correlation across patients between
      reliability scores and prediction properties, with bootstrap CIs
  No specific precedent found; standard rank correlation. Many
  correlations are computed, so they are descriptive, not tests.
  Status: descriptive, declared.

D-32  KernelSHAP run with l1_reg=False (correction, found in the audit)
  Finding [E, results/audit_report.txt A7, A7b]: shap 0.52's KernelExplainer
    by default keeps 10 features and sets the rest to exactly zero (5 of 15 on
    Framingham, 11 of 21 on BRFSS). Its error against exact Shapley values did
    not shrink with a 4x budget; with l1_reg=False it matches exact linear
    SHAP to 1e-07 and exact enumeration to 0.5% or better.
  Evidence: Lundberg & Lee 2017 [K] describe the regularisation as optional;
    Covert & Lee 2021 [V] analyse the unregularised estimator.
  Action: all Framingham KernelSHAP results regenerated (top-3 conclusions
    unchanged to 0.01); BRFSS regenerated on Kaggle.
  Status: corrected.

D-33  Validation audit (2026-10-05)
  Three audits recorded in Validation_and_Defence.txt: correctness against
  ground truth; sensitivity of conclusions to analysis choices on stored
  results; re-running of design alternatives (other splits, re-tuning,
  imputation, path-dependent TreeSHAP, subsampling, disjoint samples,
  faithfulness settings, correlated noise). D-04's complete-case check done.
  New limit on a claim: attribution SIGNS are stable (96-100%); instability
  concerns the ORDER of leading features.
  Status: done except the items marked pending in that file.

D-34  Outcome of testing the D-07 deviation (hyperparameters tuned once)
  [E, results/audit_report.txt A2 and A2b; Validation_and_Defence.txt 4.14]
  Ordinary K-fold re-tuning inside a bootstrap sample leaks, because copies of
  one row fall on both sides of a fold. XGBoost then picks its most complex
  setting in 30 of 30 bootstraps and per-patient agreement falls from 0.585 to
  0.440. With grouped folds (all copies of a row in one fold) the choices match
  those made on the real data and agreement is unchanged: LR +0.010,
  XGB -0.003, RF +0.047.
  Consequences: (1) D-07's wording "reported instability is a LOWER BOUND" is
  withdrawn; (2) Riley & Collins' advice to repeat tuning in each bootstrap
  needs grouped folds when the tuning itself uses cross-validation. No source
  was found that states this for explanation stability; it is our own check.
  Status: deviation tested; conclusion unchanged.

D-35  Replication of the sample-size experiment (D-28)
  [E, results/brfss/sample_size_report.txt; Validation_and_Defence.txt 4.15]
  2 further subsamples at 3,392 and 7,580 rows, 1 at 30,000. Mean agreement
  rises at every step for all five model/explainer rows. Between-subsample
  spread is up to about 0.10 at the two smallest sizes; results are reported as
  mean and range, not from one subsample.
  Status: replicated; still exploratory (added after the freeze).
